# Findings

This document collects what the project found, question by question, from the first exact game to the final
training campaign. Each finding links to the report that holds its measurements; those reports are in the
[archive](archive/README.md), unchanged.

Three kinds of statement are kept apart:

- **Evidence** is something measured, given with its number and its sample.
- **Interpretation** is how a report read its evidence. It names the report.
- **Decision** is something adopted or rejected, with who decided.

Values in the champion game are in the game's gold unit. One fresh wave of minions fully collected is worth 1, a
tower plate 1 and a kill 2, and a self-play agent earns about 0.39 per turn ([model](model.md) §5.1). Intervals are
95% intervals over seeds unless a section says otherwise. A "lower bound" on exploitability comes from one trained
attacker and could be raised by a stronger one.

## 1. The exact wave-management game (Phase 1)

The first game has only the minion waves, three wave actions per turn and an expected gank cost. It is small enough
to solve exactly by backward induction, which gives equilibria, exact best responses and exploitability.

**Evidence.**

- The equilibrium value at the standard start is 0 and the symmetric equilibrium is passive and crash-free in all
  three versions of the game: the original model (`GameConfig.v1()`), the farming revision (`.v2()`) and the
  default siege model (`GameConfig()`) ([v1 report](archive/phase1/phase1_implementation_report.md) §A,
  [revision](archive/phase1/phase1_revision_report.md) §A, [siege study](archive/phase1/phase1_siege_vision_study.md)
  §A). No crash appears on the equilibrium path in 61 of 61 sensitivity settings of the revision (§H).
- Simple fixed styles are very exploitable. At horizon 32 in the original model, always shoving gives up 19.45 and
  playing uniformly 11.86; a defensive freeze earns the equilibrium value against the equilibrium yet gives up 1.85
  to a best response (v1 report §F.4).
- Giving a crash something to buy changes its value. With siege objectives (tower pressure and vision bought inside
  the action) a 12-HP crash state goes from −5.79 to +6.05 for the crasher, the equilibrium establishes vision first
  (98% of siege nodes) and applies pressure second (60%), and the exploitability of the passive baseline rises from
  9.42 to 21.47 (siege study §A, §G.1, §G.3). No single mechanic rescues the crash on its own (§F).

**Interpretation.** The reports read crash-free play as structural to this stylized geometry, robust to seven
collision models and 23 parameter settings, and explicitly "not a claim about real League of Legends" (v1 report
§F.1, §F.6). The siege study reads its result as exploitable value moving from cashing in crashes to controlling the
map (§G.1).

**Decision.** The siege model became the default `GameConfig()`. Its K = 32 solution is the Phase 1 record,
summarized in `artifacts/siege/solution_K32_summary.json` and regenerated by `python -m lanegame solve`. Later work kept the exact game as a regression oracle on small horizons instead of
enlarging it, because enumerating a richer game "is not a realistic goal" ([roadmap](archive/rl_research_roadmap.md)
§A).

## 2. Learning on the first champion game (M1 to M3)

Champions (HP, trades, Recall, ganks, death) made the game too large to solve, so it is played through an
environment and learned with PPO.

**Evidence.**

- PPO beat the best scripted response against every opponent tested, reaching 23% to 25% of the exact best-response
  gain against a passive opponent and 67% to 75% against an aggressive one on the exactly solvable arena
  ([M2–M3 report](archive/rl/m2_m3_implementation_report.md) §D.2).
- Self-play against a pool of frozen snapshots did not approach the mixed equilibrium. On the exact arena its
  exploitability fell from 3.74 (uniform) to 2.52 at 20k steps, then rose to 3.43 at 205k (same report §D.3).
- The OpenAI Five recipe split into two parts with different value. One network playing both seats helped (80/20
  beat the snapshot pool in 9 of 9 seed pairings, mean +0.20); the 20% of games against historical snapshots gave
  no measurable robustness benefit and was more exploitable on the exact arena than pure self-play in all 3 seeds
  ([OpenAI Five study](archive/rl/openai_five_selfplay_report.md) §A, §F.3, §F.6).
- The diagnostics study found a one-turn Freeze identical to Slow Push in all 72 reachable Freeze-legal states, a
  late Shove from turn 28 that beat all 9 policies because the game ended at turn 32, and siege states never visited
  after 51k transitions, although 1M-step attackers found a profitable siege plan
  ([diagnostics](archive/rl/openai_five_diagnostics_report.md) §C, §D.3, §D.4, §E).

**Interpretation.** The diagnostics report classed sieges and wave timing as a training issue and Freeze as a
mechanics limitation (§D.5). The roadmap later summarized the era as "the game itself, not the learner, limited
what could be learned" (roadmap summary); the two readings differ on sieges.

**Decision.** Shared-policy self-play became the trainer of every later campaign, at 100% current-policy play
(OpenAI Five's 80/20 bought nothing again in M4.3b). The game was redesigned as one integrated, continuing-time
ruleset (M4) instead of tuning PPO. The planned PSRO stage never ran after the scope was narrowed to OpenAI
Five-style training (roadmap §D).

## 3. What the integrated game rewards (M4.1 to M4.3)

M4 added an economy (levels, items, plates, gold invested on Recall), natural minion waves and continuing time
without a terminal turn, then calibrated it with scripted play and restricted matrix games before any long training.

**Evidence.**

- Clearing the enemy wave every turn was the best reply to itself, a property of the incentives and not a PPO
  failure. In the M4.3b pilot Clear was 63% to 75% of play under two entropy settings, with and without historical
  opponents and from archive starts. Forcing only the wave component to Hold, Slow Push or a freeze lost 1.7 to 2.6
  against the policy, and a policy that never clears lost 8 to 13 resources per game against every opponent
  ([PPO pilot](archive/rl/m4_3b_ppo_pilot_report.md) §7.2, §11;
  [validation](archive/rl/m4_3b_validation_report.md) §8).
- Only the economy changed that. A smaller crash tax (a defender under its own tower keeps 0.8 of the lane's farm
  rate instead of 0.6), together with a trade factor of 0.8 instead of 0.5, kill gold 2.0 instead of 0.5 and death
  timers of 3, 4 and 5 turns, moved PPO from 70% to 75% Shove to 31% to 34% in a two-seed pilot. Every isolated
  death, kill, trade or Recall change was inert within ±0.1
  ([economic calibration](archive/m4/m4_3/m4_3c_strategic_economics_calibration.md) §0, §4.1, §10).
- Adding XP to the reward at any weight from 0 to ½ changed no equilibrium, and ending the game at the last tower
  plate gave a leader a reason not to take that plate (same report §6, §7;
  [objective design](archive/m4/m4_3/m4_3c_economic_objective_design.md) §0).
- No tested rule produced the League "stand back from the wave when threatened" concession. In 180 solved policy
  games the weaker champion never stood back; standing back cost 6 to 13 per 32 turns against 0 to 4 for contesting
  ([zoning study](archive/natural_waves/m4_2e_zoning_report.md) §3.3, §10).

**Interpretation.** The reports draw two method lessons from this era. Test whether the game rewards a strategy
before blaming the learner, and remember that fixed constants standing in for consequences can reverse incentives
(the zoning study found two fixed collection rates "doing the work zoning should do, with the wrong sign").

**Decision.** The objective fixed in M4.1 was kept, the zero-sum earned-gold difference (farm, plates and kills),
γ 0.99, continuing time, truncation only. The gold-plus-XP reward and laning termination were not adopted
([economic calibration](archive/m4/m4_3/m4_3c_strategic_economics_calibration.md) §12). Each accepted rule became an
opt-in field and a new preset; the chain is listed in [methodology](methodology.md) §2.

## 4. What PPO self-play discovered (M4.4 and M4.5)

M4.4 trained shared-policy PPO on three economies; M4.5 unified positional combat, vision and minion aggro into the
research preset `m45_calibrated()` and trained six seeds on it to 16,384,000 transitions each.

**Evidence.** Each behaviour below was shown by paired counterfactuals from restored states (the agent's own play
against fixed alternative plans on common random numbers), not by action frequencies
([M4.5c research report](archive/m4/m4_5/m4_5c_research_report.md) §5).

- Wave management depends on the state. Shove has the best immediate gold (+0.45) yet is the modal action in only
  22% of states, and one forced turn of Last Hit, Thin, Slow Push or Shove costs −0.25, −0.23, −0.19 and −0.11
  (§5.1, §5.7).
- Freezing pays when the opponent backs off (+0.56 in natural states, +1.86 in constructed ones, 6 of 6 seeds) and
  costs −0.20 ± 0.07 against the learned policies (§5.2).
- Trading is selective. A forced trade into a heavier wave costs −0.34 ± 0.16 (§5.3); M4.6b's corrected
  measurement, with positions counted in the lane only, reads about −0.08
  ([M4.6b](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6b_investment_report.md) §1.3).
- Recall is timed. Staying at low HP costs −0.89 ± 0.18 (6 of 6) (§5.5). In M4.4c, Recall was best or tied-best of
  five alternatives, and interrupted Recalls turned out to be a rational guessing game
  ([M4.4c](archive/m4/m4_4/m4_4c_strategic_investigation.md) §3).
- Some actions are rationally avoided. A forced Ward costs −0.43 ± 0.09 (6 of 6); a bully plan at a ×1.76 power gap
  gives −0.23 to +0.15 against the policy and pays +1.28 ± 0.32 only against a conceding scripted opponent (§5.3,
  §5.4).
- The policies kept improving. The 16,384,000-transition finals beat the 8,192,000 ones in all 36 pairings
  (+1.03 ± 0.11), and an annealing test showed this was data, not the learning-rate schedule (§2).
- They are exploitable. Attackers trained at a quarter of the target's budget beat their targets by +1.02 ± 0.48
  (6 of 6) and were near zero against other finals (§7).

**Interpretation.** The report answers its question ("can PPO self-play independently discover economically
meaningful League-inspired laning strategies, without explicit strategy shaping?") with "yes, conditionally", and
reads restraint against competent play as correct (§9).

**Decision.** `m45_calibrated()` is the research preset of every later study. M4.5b found and narrowly corrected an
inherited farm-spot defect in mutual Shoves (38% to 50% of Shove-mirror farm died out of reach) before selecting it
([calibration](archive/m4/m4_5/m4_5b_calibration_report.md) §8.2, §9).

## 5. The limits the diagnosis found (M4.6 to M4.8)

M4.6 asked why freezes broke cheaply and why strength rarely became denial; M4.7 and M4.8 asked how to read the lane
phase economically.

**Evidence.**

- Zoning is rewarded by the rules but missing from training. A learned gap specialist beat its target by
  +2.11 ± 0.62 at a power gap (3 of 3), while gaps of ×1.25 or more occur on only 0.07% to 0.37% of self-play turns
  ([M4.6a](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6a_report.md) §6.1, §8.3).
- Freezes break cheaply for structural reasons. A guard can hold one only while the breaker's wave capacity is at
  most 7 (through level 6 in the baseline, level 8 under C1), a window of 0.03% of turns
  ([M4.6d](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6d_report.md) §3.1; [M4 closeout](archive/m4/m4_closeout_report.md) §4.1).
- The critic is nearly blind to strength. The observation is exact and lossless, but the shared actor–critic trunk
  drops the duel ratio in the layer the value head reads (R² 1.00 from the observation, 0.18 from layer 2), and
  large gaps never occur in training ([M4.6e](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6e_report.md) §2.1, §2.3, §3). A critic with its own
  trunk recovered about a third of the strength premium (plausible-edge recovery 0.45 against 0.21, roadmap §AR),
  with no material behavioural improvement demonstrated within the tested budget
  ([M4.6f](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6f_report.md) §5, in the wording the closeout §2 corrected it to).
- A steeper investment curve (C1) made mid-game leads modestly more consequential (+0.34 ± 0.17 at a +16 investment
  gap) but its pre-registered primary result was inconclusive (+0.09 ± 0.11)
  ([M4.6c](archive/m4/m4_6/m4_6c_report.md) §4, §10).
- The lane phase is turns 0 to 93 (level 14 arrives at turn 94.3 ± 0.5). A matched pilot with a shorter horizon
  (γ 1 − 1/60) gained nothing reliable in the lane and lost against fixed opponents, pre-registered outcome 5
  ([M4.7](archive/m4/m4_7/m4_7_horizon_report.md) §1.1, §5).
- Raw gold at the end of the lane misleads. In about two thirds of natural turn-94 states the expected change over
  the next 20 turns is material (SD 1.16 against a mean lead of 3.47); reset status, the wave and HP explain it
  (R² 0.80) ([M4.8](archive/m4/m4_8/m4_8_lane_exit_report.md) §4.1, §4.2).

**Decision.** The user kept γ 0.99 and continuing time, ruled out a terminal state at level 14, adopted the lane-exit
readout E_20 as the standard lane-phase measure, chose the baseline `m45_calibrated()` over C1 and kept the shared
critic (roadmap, "Decisions of 2026-10-17"). The closeout added an evaluation rule, which is to never read
critic values as evidence in strength-gap states.

## 6. Opponent modelling (OM1 and OM2)

**Evidence.**

- The lane reveals the opponent's type early. A Bayesian belief over a library of 15 types picked the right best
  response in 96% of games within 10 turns and 99% within 20, and a switcher built from existing policies beat the
  policy by +0.90 ± 0.27 (83% of an oracle), pre-registered outcome 1
  ([OM1](archive/opponent_modelling/om1_feasibility_report.md) §3, §4). The value came mostly from scripted
  opponents (+1.36) and turned negative against stochastic style mixtures (−0.83 and −1.45) (§4, §5).
- A trained policy with the belief as input beat its matched control (belief held at the prior) by +2.03 ± 1.72
  against archetypes with no loss elsewhere, pre-registered outcome 1
  ([OM2 pilot](archive/opponent_modelling/om2_pilot_report.md) §3).

**Interpretation.** The OM2 report itself points out that "the belief is not the main source of exploitation; the
opponent distribution is": training against the styles alone gave +5.1 against archetypes, the belief the further
+2.0, and the control ended slightly below the common start against competent opponents (§5, §6).

## 7. The final campaign (M5)

M5 compared the whole opponent-modelling package (the belief, the previous action and a 50% fixed-opponent training
mix) with continued pure self-play, six seed pairs of 32,768,000 transitions per run, under a frozen
pre-registration with one amendment made before training. The [research overview](research_overview.md) explains it
for newcomers, and every number in it is checked against the records by `python -m experiments.m6d overview`.

**Evidence** ([M5 final report](archive/m5/m5e_closeout_report.md) §3 to §5).

- Pre-registered outcome 2, "pays at a competence cost". Against scripted styles the modelling arm gained
  +9.26 [+8.95, +9.56]. Against competent opponents it lost −0.63 [−0.98, −0.27], and head to head −0.44
  [−0.72, −0.16]; both losses hold in every seed.
- The gain depends on resemblance to the training library. It is +12.15 against the library's styles and about
  +11.4 against scripted variants of them, but there is no gain against conditional styles built on a competent
  policy (−0.18, −0.67, −0.20).
- Four of five strategic findings of M4.5c replicate in both arms (wave management, freezing and its reply,
  selective trading, Recall timing). Concession under threat replicates in neither, and the arms fail on different
  evidence (modelling at the registered 50 turns, pure self-play only in two seeds re-read at 150 turns)
  ([audit](archive/audit/repository_audit_2026-10-22.md) §4).
- Both arms are exploitable. Attackers lead the modelling finals by +2.96 [+0.32, +5.61] and the self-play finals by
  +1.15 [+0.34, +1.96]; the difference, +1.81 [−0.56, +4.19], is inconclusive. No attack transferred to another target.
- Pure self-play was still improving at the end (+0.59 [+0.14, +1.05] over its 75% checkpoint); the modelling arm
  was not shown to be (−0.08 [−0.40, +0.24]).

**Interpretation.** Neither arm is better overall; the package buys exploitation of recognizable styles at a
competence cost, and M5 cannot say which ingredient causes the cost (final report §6, §11).

**Decision.** Present both arms as a trade-off and never combine the gain and the cost into one score (final report
§7). The web demo plays the pair-0 finals of both arms.

## 8. Network capacity (NC1, experimental)

NC1 (2026-10-29) asked whether the 2 × 128 network, kept by convention since M3, limits the agent. Its report is
complete; the user's review is open, so its recommendation is a proposal.

**Evidence** ([NC1 report](archive/capacity/nc1_report.md) §2 to §6). A 2 × 128 student kept 98% of a gap
specialist's gain under distillation ("2 × 128 suffices"). Doubling the width did not improve value fits
(−0.001 ± 0.029) while six explicit strength features did (+0.73 ± 0.31). In PPO, the wide network lost head to head
(−0.67 ± 0.59) with 50% more KL per update, and the feature network was level (−0.17 ± 0.42) with a better critic.
Both registered verdicts are "inconclusive".

**Interpretation.** The report attributes the wide network's loss to optimization (a larger effective step under
Adam) rather than representation (§7) and proposes keeping 2 × 128 (§8).

## 9. Open questions

These remain unresolved in the records:

- What causes M5's competence cost, whether the belief input, the fixed-opponent games or the smaller share of self-play
  (M5 final report §11, §14).
- Whether pure self-play converges, and how exploitable each arm is beyond quarter-budget attackers.
- How to make power gaps, and so zoning, appear in training (M4.6a) without scripting them.
- Whether a strength-aware critic (a separate trunk or explicit features) improves behaviour when trained from the
  start (M4.6f, NC1).
- Concession under threat, freeze durability against competent breakers and late-game leads, which the M4 closeout
  lists as missed or weak (closeout §4.1).
- Play against people, which was never measured.

The [project history](archive/history.md) records how each of these questions arose, the approaches that failed
and the corrections made after the fact.
